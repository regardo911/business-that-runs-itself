# Chapter 11: the guardrails policy

This is the file that keeps the rest safe. The book's one-line rule: "Claude does the work; you approve before anything sends, posts, or pays." Your policy writes down where your own lines sit on top of that.

## What you fill in

`guardrails-policy.md`, four short lists:

| Tier | What goes in it | The book's examples |
|---|---|---|
| Green: auto-allowed | Workflows that only read and summarize | `/monday-brief`, a cash forecast, a customer themes report |
| Yellow: human-approved | Anything that sends, posts, or pays | Invoice reminders, customer replies, payments and refunds, marketing posts |
| Red: never automated | The irreversible and the human | Firing someone, signing a contract, legal or tax decisions, a refund big enough to hurt |
| Review rhythm | When you re-read the policy, and what moves a workflow to stricter approval | Monthly; any workflow that makes a mistake |

Every workflow you run goes in one tier. Most money and customer work belongs in yellow.

## How to write it

1. Open `guardrails-policy-example.md` beside your blank. It's the pool company's policy from the book. The pool company set its refund threshold at $100; pick your own number and name your own biggest account.
2. Sort every workflow you've run since chapter 4 into green, yellow or red.
3. Write your red lines in plain words: "never sign a contract, never fire or discipline anyone, never issue a refund over $____, never email ____ without my review, never make a tax decision."
4. Confirm the gates are on: run each yellow workflow once and check that it stops and shows you the draft before anything sends or pays.
5. Start strict. Approve more than you think you need to for the first month.
6. Save the policy in your `My Business Context` folder, next to the four context files.

## Keep it alive

Once a month, answer the five questions at the bottom of your policy file. Loosen one narrow yellow action at a time, and only after you've watched it get the job right many times. Money, major customers and everything red stay human-approved.


## Done when

> "Every money-touching and customer-facing action in your setup is behind an explicit approval gate, and you have a written list of the things the tool may never do on its own. If you can hand someone your red-lines doc and they'd know exactly where your automation stops, you're done."
