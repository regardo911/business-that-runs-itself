# Chapter 3: the context pack

Every workflow in the rest of the book reads these four files. Fill them in before you install anything; "set me up" then goes fast.

## Copy them out

Make a folder called `My Business Context` (on your desktop is fine). Copy these four files into it:

| File | What goes under its headings |
|---|---|
| `services-and-pricing.md` | What you sell, what it costs, your payment terms, who gets special terms |
| `my-tools.md` | Every piece of software you use and what for, plus the ones you may cancel |
| `how-we-do-things.md` | The way you actually run a job, start to finish, and what you never do |
| `brand-voice-and-numbers.md` | Three real messages you sent customers, your words, your key numbers, your busy and slow seasons |

`example-pool-company/` is the book's three-truck pool company, filled in. Open it to see what a finished file looks like. Don't copy it.

## Fill them in

Start with `services-and-pricing.md`. Real numbers, the ones you'd put on an invoice.

Stop when each file has five to ten real bullets and the brand-voice file has three real messages you actually sent. That's the book's rule. Notes, not a manual.

The last heading in `my-tools.md`, "Tools We Used To Use But May Cancel", is your head start on the chapter 9 teardown. Add to it whenever a workflow makes an app pointless.

## Hand them to Cowork

In chapter 4 you say `set me up`. Answer its questions from these files, then point it at the folder or paste the four files in.

## The quiz that tells you it worked

Once you're set up, ask Claude these five questions and check each answer against your own files:

1. What do we charge for our standard service?
2. Who are our biggest customers and what terms do they get?
3. What's our policy on starting a job without a signed estimate?
4. How do we handle an invoice that's two weeks overdue?
5. How do we sound when we text a customer?

Missed one? The gap is in your files, not the tool. Add the answer.

## Keep it current

Change the file the same day you change the business. New price, new service, new payment tool, a service you dropped: open the file, fix the line. Stale context is worse than none, because the tool uses it with full confidence.
